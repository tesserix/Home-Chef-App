<objective>
GH #885: when a refund goes out because the CHEF is at fault (they cancelled, or they
issued a post-delivery goodwill refund), the customer keeps getting refunded 100% — that
is already true and stays untouched — and the payment gateway's transaction-fee loss on
that specific refund is now recovered from the chef through the existing chef-penalty
levy mechanism (services/chef_penalty.go), the same machine that already runs the
cancel_late levy: raise → (optional grace) → admin waiver → weekly-statement deduction.

Scope is Cashfree only (the platform's preferred gateway, models.PreferredChefPaymentProvider).
Razorpay and Stripe refunds do not levy in this change; that gap is a deliberate, noted
follow-up, not an oversight — see the design-questions summary in the task actions below.

Purpose: recover a real, currently-unrecovered cost (the gateway's per-refund processing
fee) from the party whose action caused it, without ever touching what the customer
receives or bolting a second parallel penalty mechanism onto the codebase.

Output:
  - A second ChefPenalty kind (gateway_fee) raised alongside the existing cancel_late kind,
    sharing every piece of existing machinery: the model, the idempotent SourceKey pattern,
    the admin waiver endpoint, the chef-visible penalty list, and the weekly statement
    deduction — none of which need to change.
  - Four wiring points, each best-effort and each provably unable to affect the customer's
    refund: CancelOrder, CancelOrderItem, RefundOrder (all in chef_order_cancel.go), and
    InitiateRefund's Cashfree branch (payment.go, chef-initiated only).
  - CashfreeRefund gains the gateway's own reported refund_charge for observability,
    unused by the levy arithmetic (which stays a configured flat rate per the locked
    decision), but available so ops can later compare configured-rate-vs-actual.
</objective>
